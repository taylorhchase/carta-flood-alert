CARTA FLOOD ALERT SYSTEM -- HISTORICAL VALIDATION: AUGUST 2025
==========================================================================

THE EVENT
---------
On August 22, 2025, CARTA posted a generic Instagram alert reading
"service interruptions due to flooding" -- no advance warning, no
route names, no stop names, posted reactively rather than the night
before. This report reruns our actual model against NOAA's historical
records to show what it would have displayed on 2025-08-20, 48
hours before 2025-08-22.

STEP 1: NOAA OBSERVED WATER LEVELS -- AUG 20-22, 2025 (GROUND TRUTH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  2025-08-20: peak observed = 7.61 ft at 2025-08-20 19:06
  2025-08-21: peak observed = 7.72 ft at 2025-08-21 19:54
  2025-08-22: peak observed = 7.93 ft at 2025-08-22 20:30 <- CARTA's Instagram alert

This was not a single-day surprise -- it was a 3-day escalation, with
every one of these three days already at or above our MODERATE
threshold (7.5 ft) before CARTA said anything publicly.

STEP 2: WHAT OUR SYSTEM WOULD HAVE SEEN ON AUG 20 (48 HOURS OUT)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Astronomical predicted peak vs. actual observed peak, each day:
  Date        Predicted (astro)   Observed (actual)   Implied surge
  2025-08-20  6.25                7.61                1.37 ft
  2025-08-21  6.28                7.72                1.44 ft
  2025-08-22  6.23                7.93                1.70 ft

The astronomical tide barely moved day to day (~6.2-6.3 ft, unremarkable
for Charleston Harbor) while the surge component climbed steadily --
this was a growing storm surge event layered on a routine tide, exactly
the failure mode the project brief describes: tide predictions alone
would not have caught it.

Predicted PEAK for 2025-08-22, as knowable on 2025-08-20: 6.23 ft

STEP 3: NWS COASTAL FLOOD ADVISORY -- HISTORICAL AVAILABILITY CHECK
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Control query (area=SC, last 7 days): 134 alerts returned
  -> confirms the historical alerts endpoint is reachable and working.
Test query (zone=SCZ047, Aug 19-23, 2025): 0 alerts returned

HONEST LIMITATION: the same query pattern returns real alerts for the
past week but zero for a 13-month-old date -- that's a retention
cutoff in NWS's public archive, not proof that no advisory was issued.
Given three straight days of 7.5+ ft observed flooding, an active
Coastal Flood Advisory or Warning during this window is likely, but
this API cannot confirm it retroactively. The LIVE dashboard is not
affected by this gap -- it reads ACTIVE alerts in real time, which
only a historical backtest like this one is blind to.

STEP 4: WHAT OUR MODEL WOULD HAVE DECLARED ON AUG 20 (TIDE-ONLY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted peak 6.23 ft -> classify_severity() = NONE

Because Step 3 could not recover a historical surge figure, and the
astronomical tide alone falls below our 7.0 ft MINOR threshold, a
tide-prediction-only run on Aug 20 would NOT have flagged this event.
This is precisely why carta_alert.py combines tide AND active NWS
surge data rather than relying on tide predictions alone.

WHAT CARTA ACTUALLY COMMUNICATED (Aug 22, 2025):
  A single reactive Instagram post: "service interruptions due to
  flooding." No route names, no stop names, no advance timing.

WHAT THIS SYSTEM COULD PROVIDE:
  - A specific, quantified water-level estimate, 48 hours ahead.
  - Had a real-time NWS coastal flood advisory been active and ingested
    the way the live dashboard does (this historical backtest cannot
    confirm one existed, per Step 3) -- named routes, named stops at
    specific elevations, and a rider-ready alert, the night before.
  - Regardless of surge data availability, three straight days of
    7.5+ ft observed flooding (Step 1) is exactly the kind of pattern
    a system checking recent observed levels -- not just forward
    predictions -- could have flagged starting Aug 20 itself; that
    check is not yet part of carta_alert.py and is a natural next step.
